Help & how-to

Everything Converse does, and how to use it. Click a section to expand.

🚀 Getting started

Demo (no setup): on Home, click Demo · strong call to watch the areas fill green, or Demo · rough call to see the copilot nudge you. Demos replay a scripted call in under a minute — no keys, no mic.

Go live: add a Deepgram API key in Settings (and an Anthropic or OpenAI key for sharper questions), then click Go live. Allow your microphone (that's you), and share your meeting tab with audio (that's the prospect). Your two voices are transcribed on separate channels, so a rep asking about budget never marks budget covered.

🟢 The live overlay
  • The 8 dots (left): your qualification areas. grey = not covered, amber = partial, green = covered. The most overdue area pulses. Click any dot to see its evidence, confidence, and an “Ask about this now” button.
  • Score (e.g. 3/8) and talk meter (you vs prospect) update live in the top bar.
  • Coaching stats (top-left): questions asked and your speaking pace (wpm).
  • Suggestion card (bottom): a single bridging question at a natural pause — or instantly when you hit “Ask now”. Auto-dismisses after 25s.
  • Objection card (amber, bottom-right): pops when the prospect objects, with the matching battlecard.
  • Transcript / Notes buttons open side panels. Notes are saved into the summary.

Hotkeys: Esc close/dismiss/end · S snooze the nudged area 5 min · T transcript · N notes.

🎯 Live coaching

Coaching watches how you run the call and nudges you in real time:

  • Monologue — you've been talking too long without a pause; ask a question.
  • Talk ratio — you're talking too much of the time; let them talk.
  • Too few questions — get them talking with an open question.

The summary shows your talk ratio, questions asked, pace, and longest monologue (amber = worth improving).

🎧 Call recordings

On a live call, Converse records the audio — your mic + the shared tab audio, mixed — to a .webm file. It's saved locally under data/recordings/ and, if Drive sync is on, uploaded to Recordings/ in your Drive database. The call summary (and any call you reopen from History) shows an inline audio player.

Recording happens in the browser, so grant the mic + screen-share permissions when prompted. Demos aren't recorded (there's no live audio).

☁️ Cloud — Google Drive as your call database

Connect Google Drive (Settings → Google Drive) and turn on Sync finished calls to Google Drive. Every finished call is then filed in Drive by type: Recordings/, Transcripts/, Summaries/, Analyses/, and Sessions/ (the raw record), one file per call keyed by call id.

Open Cloud to browse everything stored in Drive. For each call you can View analysis (fetch the analysis previously saved to Drive) or Refresh analysis (re-run the AI debrief and overwrite it in Drive). A recording with no transcript yet — e.g. an audio file you dropped into Recordings/ — gets a Transcribe & analyze button that runs it through Deepgram's pre-recorded transcription first, then analyzes it (needs a Deepgram key). Drive is the source of truth, so your analyses live with your data — portable and re-runnable anytime.

🏅 Scorecard & skill index

Scorecard rolls up every saved call into a skill profile: Discovery & coverage (how much you qualify), Listening (talk ratio — best near a 45% rep share), Questioning (8+ good questions is strong), and Objection handling (share of objections you had a battlecard ready for). Each rep gets an overall grade, a trend, and a clickable recent-calls list (jumps to that call's summary).

Set Settings → Rep name to tag your calls. Use different names (e.g. per teammate) to get a team roll-up across everyone. It's all computed from your saved calls — no extra tracking.

📋 Pre-call brief & 📅 Calendar

Open Pre-call before you dial, type who you're meeting (and optionally the account), and hit Generate brief. You get a one-screen prep: a suggested opening line, a recap of your last call with that contact (coverage, what's still open, objections, competitors named, notes), a suggested agenda that puts open qualification gaps first, and the objections you're most likely to hit based on your history.

It's built entirely from your saved calls, so it works offline. With an Anthropic or OpenAI key set, one AI pass sharpens the agenda, opener, and likely objections (look for the ✨ AI-sharpened badge).

From your calendar: connect Google (Settings) and your upcoming meetings appear at the top of Pre-call — click one and its title + attendees prefill the account/persona and generate the brief. One Google sign-in grants both Drive and Calendar; if you connected Drive before this feature existed, reconnect once to grant calendar access.

🧠 AI deal debrief (post-call)

The moment a call ends, Converse runs an AI debrief over the transcript and your qualification coverage and drops it at the top of the summary:

  • What went well / what didn't / what to improve — an honest read on how the call ran.
  • Missed opportunities and red flags — where you could have dug deeper, and risks to the deal.
  • Budget & key decision points — economics, decision criteria, process, and people that matter.
  • Sentiment — an overall read (positive / neutral / negative) with a one-line rationale.
  • Follow-up email — a ready-to-send draft; hit Copy to paste it into your mail client.

With an Anthropic or OpenAI key set, this is a single LLM pass. With no key (or in demo mode) you still get a solid deterministic debrief derived from coverage, objections, and talk ratio. The debrief is saved into the summary Markdown and every export.

⚠️ Objection detection & objection trees

When the prospect raises a price, timing, competitor, authority, skepticism, or status-quo objection, an amber card surfaces the most relevant battlecard from your Context docs — plus a numbered objection tree: a multi-step response flow (acknowledge → reframe → evidence → advance) with ready-to-say lines, so you don't just get a doc, you get a play.

To make the battlecards useful, add them in the Context tab (e.g. “battlecard-vs-workday”). Objections raised are listed in the summary.

🏁 Competitive intel & case-study surfacing

When the prospect names a competitor, a blue card pops up with your head-to-head battlecard. When they ask for proof — a case study, a reference, ROI, "customers like us" — the card surfaces the most relevant case study / proof point instead.

Competitors are recognized from a built-in list, anything you add under Settings → Competitors, and names inferred from your battlecard filenames (so battlecard-vs-workday teaches it "Workday" automatically). Add case studies in Context (e.g. “case-study-northwind”). Everything surfaced is listed in the summary under In-call intelligence.

🔀 Frameworks (MEDDPICC / BANT / SPICED)

By default the copilot tracks MEDDPICC (8 areas). In Settings → Framework switch live calls to BANT (Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline) or SPICED — the rail, time budgets, and classifier all adapt. (Demos always use MEDDPICC.)

📄 Context docs

Drop your battlecards, product one-pagers, ICP notes, and pricing into the Context tab (Markdown, one topic per file). The copilot pulls the most relevant doc into each suggested question and matches battlecards to objections — so guidance is grounded in your product, not generic.

🎛️ Persona, models & custom prompts
  • AI provider — use Anthropic (Claude) or OpenAI (GPT). Paste the matching API key and pick the provider; the model names auto-adjust if you don't set your own.
  • Persona — set who you're selling to (e.g. “CFO — ROI & risk”) so questions match what they care about.
  • Models & cooldown — choose the model and how often nudges may fire.
  • Custom prompts — override the classifier and question prompts entirely (blank = built-in; the default is shown as a placeholder to copy).
📊 Summary, exports & dashboard

After a call you get a summary: coverage grid with the exact prospect quote behind each area, talk ratio, coaching stats, objections, notes, and what's still open. Export it with:

  • Copy / Copy CRM fields — to clipboard.
  • Download / Transcript — Markdown files.
  • Email — a pre-filled draft · Slack — posts to your webhook (set in Settings) · Drive — uploads to Google Drive.

The Dashboard aggregates all calls: average coverage, per-area cover rate, a coverage trend, and overall talk ratio.

⚡ Real-time performance
  • Instant nudges — while the prospect is talking, the copilot pre-generates the question for the most-overdue area, so it appears the instant they pause (the ⚡ in the top-bar readout means it was served with zero wait).
  • Faster coverage — the classifier re-scores right after the prospect finishes a thought (not on a fixed timer), caches its static prompt, and only sends new transcript — so dots update in seconds.
  • Echo guard — if your mic bleeds into the prospect's audio channel, those echoed lines are dropped so your own words never fake coverage.
  • Latency readout — the top bar shows live classifier / nudge latency; the summary reports the averages. Great for spotting a slow model or network.
🗑️ Your data (save, backup, delete)

Every call is saved locally (nothing leaves your machine except what you export to your own keys/Drive). In History you can delete one call, Export backup (.json), Export & wipe all, or Delete all. Wiping can't be undone — keep a backup if you might want the calls later.

💾 Google Drive & Slack setup

Drive (sign in from the app): in Google Cloud create an OAuth client (Desktop app) and download its JSON. Then, in Settings → Google Drive, paste that JSON once and click Connect Google Drive — you'll sign in with Google in your browser, no .env and no terminal. (You can still set GOOGLE_OAUTH_CLIENT in .env, or use npm run connect-drive, if you prefer.)

Google requires that one-time OAuth client — there's no way around it — but only one person sets it up (paste or bundle it once); after that every user just clicks Connect and signs in. Personal Gmail accounts will see a one-time "unverified app → continue" screen; that's expected for a self-hosted app.

Slack: create an incoming webhook and paste its URL into Settings → Slack webhook URL. Then use Slack on the summary.
